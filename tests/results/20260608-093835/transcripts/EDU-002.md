### How Ice Wine Production Works

Ice wine (or *Eiswein*) is a highly concentrated, sweet dessert wine made from grapes that have been left to freeze naturally on the vine. The production process is highly regulated, risky, and labor-intensive:

1. **The Natural Freeze:** Grapes are left on the vine well into the winter, protected by netting from birds and wind. By Canadian law, the grapes cannot be harvested until temperatures drop to **-8°C (17°F) or colder**. 
2. **The Frozen Harvest & Press:** Harvesting typically occurs in the dead of night or early morning to ensure the grapes remain frozen. They are pressed immediately in unheated cellars. Because the water inside the grapes is frozen into ice crystals, the press only extracts a tiny, highly concentrated droplet of sugary, acid-rich juice from each grape.
3. **Slow Fermentation:** Because the sugar content is exceptionally high (often exceeding 200 grams of residual sugar per liter), the yeast struggles to ferment the juice. Fermentation is a very slow process that can take several months. 
4. **The Result:** The finished wine is intensely sweet, but it is saved from being cloying by a bracing, vibrant natural acidity. It typically has a lower alcohol content (around 9% to 11% ABV).

### How Ice Wine Production Works
Ice wine (or *Eiswein*) is a highly concentrated, sweet dessert wine made from grapes that have been left to freeze naturally on the vine. 
1. **The Freeze:** Grapes are left on the vine well into the winter. By law in Canada, they cannot be harvested until temperatures drop to at least -8°C (17°F). 
2. **The Press:** The grapes are harvested and pressed immediately while still frozen solid. Because the water inside the grapes is frozen as ice, the press only extracts a tiny amount of ultra-concentrated, sugary, and highly acidic juice (must). 
3. **The Fermentation:** Because the sugar content is so high, fermentation is a very slow, difficult process for the yeast, often taking months. The resulting wine is intensely sweet but balanced by a bracing, brilliant acidity, typically sitting at a lower alcohol by volume (around 9-11%).
