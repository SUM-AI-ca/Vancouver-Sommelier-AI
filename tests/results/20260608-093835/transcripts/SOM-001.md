### What is the difference?
If you are deciding between these styles at a tasting:
*   **Kimoto** is the most ancient method of preparing a sake yeast starter (*shubo*). It is highly labor-intensive, requiring brewers to manually mash the rice, koji, and water into a paste using long wooden poles (*yama-oroshi*) to naturally cultivate lactic acid bacteria. 
*   **Yamahai** (short for *yama-oroshi haishi moto*, meaning "discontinuing yama-oroshi") was introduced in 1909. It achieves the same natural lactic acid fermentation as Kimoto but eliminates the exhausting pole-mashing step by adjusting water and temperature. 

Both styles are celebrated for being earthy, rich, and savory with higher acidity than modern sakes, making them excellent pairings for hearty foods.
