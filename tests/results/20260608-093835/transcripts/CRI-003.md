### **Alternative Pairings for a Cabernet-Friendly Meal**
If you are serving a rich, red-meat dish that calls for a wine like Burrowing Owl, here are some excellent alternatives across other categories:

**Beer**
*   **Recommendation:** **Strange Fellows 'Boris' Russian Imperial Stout** or **Brassneck 'Inertia' Imperial Stout** (both Vancouver, BC).
*   **Why it works:** The roasted barley, dark chocolate, and espresso notes in these stouts mimic the dark fruit and oak spice of a big Cabernet. The high ABV and rich mouthfeel provide the necessary weight to stand up to heavy proteins and rich pan sauces.

**Spirit / Cocktail**
*   **Recommendation:** A classic **Old Fashioned** made with **Odd Society 'Commodore' Single Malt Whisky** (Vancouver, BC) or **Sons of Vancouver 'Cigarettes in Space' Rye** (North Vancouver, BC).
*   **Why it works:** The Commodore Single Malt brings a beautiful peppery, dark-fruit richness, while the Sons of Vancouver Rye offers a spicy, robust backbone. Stirred down with a touch of Demerara syrup and Angostura bitters, the cocktail provides the tannin-like structure and warming finish you would expect from a big Okanagan red.

**Sake**
*   **Recommendation:** **Tengumai Yamahai Junmai** (Ishikawa, Japan) or a locally crafted robust option from **Artisan SakeMaker** (Granville Island, Vancouver).
*   **Why it works:** While sake is often associated with lighter fare, the traditional *Yamahai* brewing method allows for wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria to develop, resulting in a gamier, earthier, and highly acidic sake. Served warm or at room temperature, it acts much like a structured red wine, cutting through the fat of a ribeye while amplifying the savory umami notes of the meat.
