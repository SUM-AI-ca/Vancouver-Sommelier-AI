Pairing a wine with a grilled ribeye steak is one of the most rewarding culinary matches you can make. Because a ribeye is a highly marbled, rich, and flavorful cut of beef, it requires a wine with specific structural elements to balance the fat and complement the smoky char from the grill.

### The Pairing Science
* **Tannins & Fat:** The rich marbling and dense proteins of a ribeye bind to the tannins in red wine. This interaction softens the wine's astringency on your palate while simultaneously cutting through the slickness of the beef fat, cleansing your palate between bites.
* **Acidity:** High acidity in a wine acts as a refreshing lift, preventing the rich steak from feeling too heavy.
* **Flavor Bridges:** The smoky, caramelized crust created by the grill's Maillard reaction pairs beautifully with wines that have complementary notes of oak, smoke, tobacco, black pepper, or dark cocoa.

A grilled ribeye is one of the most luxurious, heavily marbled cuts of beef you can cook. When it hits the grill, the Maillard reaction creates a savory, smoky, caramelized crust. 

**The Pairing Science:** 
To pair successfully with a ribeye, a beverage needs two primary structural elements: **tannin/bitterness** and **acidity**. The rich, slick fats and dense proteins in the steak bind to the tannins in red wine (or the hop bitterness in beer), softening the drink's astringency while simultaneously scrubbing the palate clean. Acidity provides a refreshing lift, keeping the pairing from feeling too heavy. Finally, the smoky char of the grill calls for a "flavor bridge"—drinks with complementary notes of oak, smoke, tobacco, roasted malt, or black pepper.

Here are specific recommendations across all categories, prioritizing standout British Columbia producers alongside international classics.
